01 / 05THE PERSON BEHIND THE WORK
A LITTLE ABOUT ME

Engineering is a way of thinking.

I didn't get into data engineering just to move information from one system to another. What interests me is understanding the full picture: where data comes from, why a process works the way it does, and how a better design can make a real difference.

My background is in Software Engineering, and my professional work has taken me into large, sometimes highly regulated environments. I have worked across the lifecycle of data systems: discovery, pipeline development, migration, quality validation, performance analysis and the practical challenges of keeping production workloads dependable.

I am especially drawn to problems with incomplete documentation and complex legacy code. I like reconstructing what is happening before choosing how to improve it. Python, PySpark and distributed processing are everyday tools; increasingly, I also use AI as a practical engineering aid for understanding code, investigating dependencies and exploring new approaches.

Alongside industry projects, I have pursued academic research in NLP, large language models and multimodal systems. These two perspectives complement each other: production engineering teaches rigor; research teaches curiosity. I'm interested in roles where I can keep combining both.

01 ENERGY SECTOR · ENTERPRISE PLATFORM
Data discovery · architecture · AI-assisted analysis

Untangling a legacy data ecosystem.

↗

Modernizing an enterprise forecasting data platform begins before any migration script is written. The first challenge is understanding what is actually there: hundreds of gigabytes, heterogeneous assets, MATLAB workflows and undocumented dependencies that must be mapped before making architectural decisions.

500+ GBLegacy data assessed
2,000+MATLAB files analyzed
3Engineering dimensions

The problem

A legacy repository supporting forecasting workflows combined .mat, .txt and .csv files with thousands of MATLAB-based ETL scripts. Data lineage, external integrations and many processing rules were not documented in a consolidated, readily usable form.

My contribution

I developed custom Python analyzers for structural discovery, file inspection, dependency reconstruction and workflow classification. I also explored lightweight, locally executed LLMs on CPU to assist with semantic analysis of MATLAB code and flag API, authentication and source-system references for review.

Read the technical approach+

01 · Discovery and reverse engineering

Python-based examination of heterogeneous datasets and legacy ETL assets, focused on reconstructing how data moves, where processes depend on other systems and which pipelines may be redundant or candidates for consolidation.

02 · AI as an engineering accelerator

Local LLM experimentation supported the analysis of a repository exceeding 2,000 MATLAB files. The models assisted investigation; findings still required technical verification. The objective was to improve system understanding, not replace engineering judgment.

03 · Modernization design

The architectural work considers target schemas, ETL rationalization, infrastructure needs, Databricks integration and migration toward Parquet-based storage, alongside source-to-target validation and consistency checks.

Scope and delivery status

Discovery and technical assessment are described as performed work. Data conversion, new Python ingestions, UAT and production cutover are presented as modernization scope or design considerations—not as completed deliverables.

02 PUBLIC SECTOR · ENVIRONMENTAL DATA
Distributed computing · production pipelines

Making critical data processing more scalable.

Developing and evolving data pipelines that support environmental and air-quality information systems, where scale, traceability and reliable reporting matter.

The problem

Legacy ETL processes needed to be maintained and modernized while preserving the integrity of high-volume information used by analytics, reporting and regulatory workflows.

My contribution

I designed, implemented and optimized PySpark ETL workloads in Cloudera/YARN, migrated Pentaho processes to distributed Spark pipelines, and integrated data across Oracle, HDFS, Hive and HBase. The work included data validation, lineage awareness, bottleneck analysis and processing datasets containing tens of millions of records.

Read the technical approach+

The migration was not a mechanical code translation: it required understanding existing transformation rules, designing the equivalent distributed execution strategy, validating source-to-target results and improving maintainability. Operational priorities included reliable repeatability, controlled resource usage and clear data-quality checks.
